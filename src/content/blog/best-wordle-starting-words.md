Every Wordle player has a favorite opener, and most of them are defending it with vibes. Let's do it with data instead.

## What makes an opener good?

A first guess can't be "right" very often; there are roughly 2,300 possible answers. Its real job is **information**: each letter you test either lights up (in the word) or goes gray (eliminated). A good opener maximizes how much of the answer space it cuts away, whatever the response.

That gives us three rules:

1. **Five different letters.** A duplicate letter wastes a slot. Every strong opener comes from the pool of [5-letter words with no repeated letters](/5-letter-words/with-no-repeated-letters/).
2. **Common letters first.** E, A, R, O, T, L, I, S and N appear in far more answers than J, Q, X or Z. Testing SOARE tells you more than testing JUMPY.
3. **Letters in likely positions.** S is a common *first* letter; E is a common *last* letter. An opener whose letters sit in their statistically favorite slots collects green tiles, not just yellow ones.

## The short list

Applying those rules to the answer pool, a few openers consistently rank at the top:

- **SLATE**: five common letters, S up front, E at the end. Arguably the best all-rounder.
- **CRANE**: famously recommended by solver bots; strong positional placement of R, A, N, E.
- **ADIEU**: tests four vowels at once. You'll learn a lot about the vowel skeleton, at the cost of weak consonant coverage. (It has cousins on the [four-vowel words list](/5-letter-words/with-four-vowels/).)
- **ROAST / RAISE / AROSE**: different rearrangements of the same high-value letters; pick the one your fingers like.

## The two-guess system

A single opener can't cover everything, so many strong players memorize a **pair** of openers with zero overlapping letters; for example, SLATE followed by CORNY, or CRANE followed by GUILD. Two such words test ten distinct letters, and after them the answer is usually pinned down to a handful of candidates.

This is exactly where a frequency-sorted list earns its keep: pull up [5-letter words](/5-letter-words/), filter by what you know, and the surviving candidates appear **in order of likelihood**, because Wordle answers are everyday words, not tournament obscurities.

## When the board gets weird

Two situations break the standard playbook:

- **Repeated letters.** If your yellows refuse every position you try, suspect a double letter. The [double-letter words list](/5-letter-words/with-double-letters/) shows how common LL, EE, SS and OO endings really are.
- **No vowels lighting up.** Rare, but real. Check the [words without vowels](/5-letter-words/without-vowels/) before you panic. (Spoiler: Y is doing the work.)

## The honest conclusion

The difference between the #1 opener and the #20 opener is small, maybe a tenth of a guess on average. What actually lowers your score is what you do on guesses two through four: eliminating letters systematically and checking candidates in frequency order. Pick a good opener, pick a zero-overlap partner for it, and let the sorted lists do the rest.
