Most Wordle advice is a pile of disconnected tips. What actually lowers your average is a **system**: the same four steps, every day, with the thinking front-loaded so the 11 PM you can run it on autopilot.

## Step 1: A fixed opener

Don't improvise your first guess. Pick one strong opener (SLATE, CRANE, RAISE, whichever suits you) and play it every single day. (The reasoning and the runner-ups are in our [starting words deep-dive](/blog/best-wordle-starting-words/).) Improvised openers leak information because they repeat letters or test rare ones.

## Step 2: A disjoint second word

Unless the first guess practically solved the puzzle (three-plus greens), play a **pre-chosen second word that shares zero letters with your opener**. SLATE → CORNY. CRANE → GUILD. After two guesses you've tested ten distinct letters, usually all but one or two of the letters in the answer.

The common objection: "but I had two yellows, shouldn't I chase them?" Usually not on guess two. Chasing two yellows through 26 positions wastes guesses; eliminating half the alphabet doesn't.

## Step 3: Candidates in frequency order

Now the crucial habit. List the words that fit everything you know, and check them **in order of how common they are**, not alphabetically. Wordle answers are drawn from everyday vocabulary; when both POINT and PINOT fit, POINT is the answer far more often.

This is the entire reason our [5-letter word lists](/5-letter-words/) are frequency-sorted. Filter by your greens and yellows, and the first surviving row is usually the play.

## Step 4: Endgame discipline

Guesses four and five are where streaks die, almost always to one of two traps:

**The rhyme trap.** You have _IGHT and four candidates: LIGHT, MIGHT, NIGHT, FIGHT. Guessing them one by one can burn every remaining turn. Instead, spend one guess on a word that tests several of the distinguishing letters at once: FILMY tests F, L and M in a single move. One "wasted" guess that splits four candidates beats three hopeful ones.

**The duplicate blind spot.** When nothing fits, a repeated letter is hiding from you. ERROR, LEVEL, SASSY. Scan the [double-letter list](/5-letter-words/with-double-letters/) mentally before surrendering a guess.

## Hard mode, briefly

Hard mode (revealed hints must be reused) forbids the Step 4 splitting trick, which is exactly why it's hard. The compensation: lean even more on frequency order, and prefer openers heavy on vowels. You can't afford exploratory guesses later, so buy your vowel information early with something like ADIEU from the [four-vowel list](/5-letter-words/with-four-vowels/).

## The realistic ceiling

With this system, a 3.8–4.0 average is achievable without memorizing anything beyond two openers. Below 3.6 requires genuine letter-frequency intuition and some luck. Past that, you're competing with solver bots, and they're welcome to it. The system's real win is different: you'll almost never lose, because losses come from improvisation, and you've designed the improvisation out.
